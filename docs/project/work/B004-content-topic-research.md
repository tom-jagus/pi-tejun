# Content-topic research

## Outcome

Propose worthwhile subjects and angles for the user's public content, initially
focused on streams. Use the user's interests, brand identity, audience, previous
content, and practical constraints. Keep editorial research and selections in
`content-production`.

## Why

A newsworthy development is not necessarily a good subject to demonstrate or
discuss. Content candidates should explain what an audience would gain and what
the user could realistically prepare, rather than supply a generic topic list.

## Boundaries

Pi Tejun owns the repeatable workflow; `content-production` owns the editorial
material and decisions. Consult authoritative brand guidance without creating
another version of the user's identity. Personal memory implementation remains
outside this project.

Streams are the initial focus, not a permanent restriction. The intended scope
also includes standalone videos, articles, social posts, and promotional material.
Do not require every topic to produce every format, automatically prepare every
candidate, or treat an agent recommendation as a user selection.

## Acceptance criteria

- Candidates identify an angle, intended audience benefit, supporting sources,
  and a plausible discussion or demonstration.
- Recommendations account for the user's interests, brand, audience,
  preparation constraints, and existing content, with missing context explicit.
- Each candidate explains preparation effort, significant uncertainty, and why
  its proposed format is suitable rather than assuming popularity is enough.
- Prior selections and declined candidates inform later research, with reasons
  when an earlier topic becomes worth reconsidering.
- Candidate research, agent recommendations, and user selections remain
  distinguishable in `content-production`.
- Related material can share research without committing the user to additional
  publications or starting preparation without the agreed selection authority.

## Open questions

- Which audience, editorial preferences, and practical constraints guide the
  initial stream-focused research?
- What should the agent shortlist, and what requires the user's selection?
- How should previous publications and declined ideas influence suggestions?
- What information connects a digest finding to a content candidate without
  duplicating authoritative research or decisions?
