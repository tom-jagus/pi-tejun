# Selected-topic preparation

## Outcome

Turn a selected content topic into a sourced, reviewable preparation package in
`content-production`. Initially support stream research, demonstration planning,
and an appropriate outline or script. Keep related material for other chosen
formats close to the same subject and sources.

## Why

Selecting a worthwhile topic does not make it ready to present. Preparation
should connect evidence, audience value, practical demonstrations, and the
user's intended message without reconstructing the earlier research.

## Boundaries

Use an explicit selection or other agreed approval as the trigger, rather than
preparing all candidates on a timer. Topics may come from B004 or a direct user
choice; a complete discovery pipeline is not a prerequisite.

Prepare the formats actually requested. A stream need not have a word-for-word
script, and related blog or social drafts are not automatic obligations.
Research does not authorize installing software, executing unfamiliar code,
publishing, or inventing personal experience. Technical streaming setup remains
owned by `stream-studio`.

## Acceptance criteria

- The result identifies the selected topic, requested format, audience benefit,
  and main question or message, with links to relevant prior research.
- Significant factual claims are traceable to sources; uncertainties and facts
  needing verification are explicit.
- Preparation suits the selected format and includes an understandable
  structure, with demonstration needs and practical risks where applicable.
- Claims of personal testing or results are supported by actual evidence,
  rather than generated to complete the narrative.
- Related deliverables can reuse sources and context while remaining separately
  identifiable and reviewable.
- Drafts remain in `content-production` for user review and do not imply
  approval to execute demonstrations or publish content.

## Open questions

- How is a selection expressed, and what preparation does it authorize?
- What initial output is most useful for live streams: research notes, a
  run-of-show, speaking prompts, a script, or a combination?
- How should practical tests be requested and their results incorporated?
- Which related formats should be supported first, if any, beyond the initial
  stream preparation?
